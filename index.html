Business starting point

Approval is not answerability.

Approval asks whether someone allowed an action. The accountability axis asks a different question: when the action binds a customer, counterparty, or regulator, is a natural person on record as the party who bears that obligation? An agent cannot become that party merely because a person clicked “approve”.

The decisive check

Is a person on record as the party to this action?

This is not an approval and not an audit-log entry. The record identifies a natural person, their capacity, their acknowledgement of party status, and the exact parameters to which that acknowledgement is bound.

“Approve this action”

A person permitted an agent to proceed. Nobody is thereby recorded as the signatory, declarant, fiduciary, payer, or consenting party.

BLOCK
“I am the party to this action”

A named person acknowledged that capacity, for these parameters, in a record outside the agent’s control.

can PROCEED

The gate consults this record only where α resolves to human_only. The action tab makes the distinction editable field by field.

Two independent questions

Do not make accountability a severity label.

κ
Consequence axis

How much autonomy is appropriate, given reversibility and consequence?

α
Accountability axis

May the agent be the actor at all, or must a person be the party to the obligation?

The gate takes the stricter result. A low-consequence, recoverable NDA can still require human ownership because the signature binds a party; changing its risk score does not change that fact.
What the gate asks the record to show
1
Classify outside the agent’s reach

Each reachable action type is declared delegable or human_only in a source the agent cannot author, select, or write.

2
Keep the two axes separate

κ remains the consequence ladder. α may raise the gate; a runtime approval, override, policy mode, or model output may not lower it.

3
For a human-only action, establish the party

Show who is bound, in what capacity, with their acknowledgement and a binding to these exact parameters. For a workflow, establish the extent before the first untrusted read.

An action has the class answerability = human-only if and only if its consequence requires the existence of a subject capable of answering for it to a third party — to accept a claim, give an explanation, or bear the consequence.

05Rules, evidence states, and limits

This tab is the technical rationale behind the interactive paths: the two axes, PASS / FAIL / UNESTABLISHED, property coverage, the AISVS relation, and what this specification deliberately does not decide.

The four quadrants (theory, Table 4)

κ answers “how much autonomy may this receive?”; α answers “may this be delegated to an agent at all?”. Neither reduces to the other. Click a cell to load it into the action tab.

On the reference model alone, required_oversight = max(oversight(reversibility), oversight(consequence)). For a reversible or externally reversible action, only consequence CRITICAL reaches HUMAN_OWNS: an NDA signature graded recoverable_local + LOW lands at SUPERVISED; graded strictly, externally_recoverable, at APPROVAL_REQUIRED — an approver, not a party. The property that makes it non-delegable — a party becomes bound — has no input into that function. α is that input.

Three results in the record, two outcomes at the gate

PASS
Positive evidence that the check holds: the declaration is outside agent control; the person is on record as the party; the extent was fixed externally before the first untrusted read.
FAIL
Positive evidence that it does not: the agent wrote or selected the declaration; a role, organisation, or the agent is named as the party; the agent performed the binding step; the extent was fixed after the read.
UNESTABLISHED
Not enough evidence to say: no declaration, no record, unknown placement of the store, missing timestamps, or no acknowledgement.

The gate collapses the three to two: PASS proceeds; FAIL and UNESTABLISHED take the closed path. An implementation whose vocabulary cannot tell UNESTABLISHED from FAIL fails the corpus on exact comparison (α-P9): “not known” and “known wrong” are different findings for the auditor even though both close the gate.

Nine properties, and how many cases exercise each

    Relation to OWASP AISVS C9.2

    AISVS 1.01 C9.2 already requires a trusted classification of actions (9.2.3), approvals bound to parameters, requester, context and a nonce (9.2.8), a chain fold established outside the agent’s control (9.2.10), and re-approval of extended chains (9.2.11). Its classification has one axis: consequence. This document supplies the second axis as an attribute beside 9.2.3, an independent fold beside 9.2.10, and a glossary term — the proposal of OWASP/AISVS#1154, published independently so that it can be cited and tested before a later cycle. The public C9 action-class reference model (Mayur Agnihotri, v1.2.0) is used unchanged for κ; its Action-Class Gate Lab is the companion for that axis.

    Known limits (SPEC.md §12)

    • The semantics of a declaration are trusted to the declarer. The gate can show that sign_nda is declared human_only outside the agent; it cannot show that a type declared delegable never creates an obligation.
    • Whether a composition of individually delegable actions can create an obligation is open; the fold here is monotone in the declared classes only.
    • Staleness of declarations is a separate axis (AISVS #1152) and is not graded here; the κ-side STALE verdict stays visible in the record (P9-03).
    • Records, not execution: the lab evaluates what a run recorded. Whether the runtime actually enforced the record is the auditor’s step 4 (§9.2).
